Adjustable Bed Functions Worth Specifying in a B2B Program

By Kang Cai Smart Sleep · Published November 1, 2025

Quick Summary

A buyer-focused look at adjustable bed functions: which suit different markets and programs, and which to confirm with your supplier before quotation.

When sourcing adjustable beds for OEM or distribution programs, the feature list can feel overwhelming. Zero-gravity, massage, app control, wall hugger, USB ports — not every feature adds value to every market. This guide helps B2B buyers identify which features matter for their program and what to confirm with their supplier before ordering.

Core Functions: Head and Foot Lift

Every adjustable bed frame should offer independent head and foot lift as standard. Backrest typically raises to 60° and footrest to 40°. These are the minimum functions for any adjustable bed program. If a supplier quotes a product without both, it is not a full adjustable base.

Zero-Gravity Preset

The zero-gravity position — head raised, knees elevated, spine decompressed — is the most requested preset in premium adjustable bed programs. It is now a standard expectation in North American and Scandinavian markets. Kang Cai's KR003 and KR004 smart beds include a one-touch zero-gravity preset. If your market expects this feature, confirm it is a hardware preset (not just marketing language) and that the position angles are fixed in the controller firmware.

Motor Selection: Standard vs. OKIN

Motor quality determines long-term reliability. Standard lift motors are suitable for most residential programs. German OKIN motors — known for quieter operation, tighter tolerances, and longer endurance ratings — are specified by buyers targeting premium retail or hospitality programs. Kang Cai offers OKIN motors on KD005-A1 and KD008-A1. OKIN programs typically require higher MOQs; confirm availability and pricing at quotation stage.

Massage and Vibration Functions

Massage and vibration functions add perceived value in consumer-facing retail programs but add cost and complexity. For entry-level or mid-tier programs, massage is often skipped. For premium smart bed programs, dual-zone vibration massage (head zone and foot zone independent) is standard. Confirm whether massage is motorized or vibration-based and whether it is zone-independent.

App and Remote Control Options

Wired remotes are becoming rare. Wireless RF remotes are standard. Bluetooth app control is now expected in mid-to-premium range products in most developed markets. Wi-Fi integration (for smart home compatibility) is available on selected models. Confirm what protocols are supported and whether the app is white-label available for your brand.

USB Charging Ports

USB charging ports (Type-A and Type-C) in the bed frame are a low-cost addition with high perceived value in consumer markets. Confirm port placement (headboard or side rail), number of ports, and whether they are included in the base price or as an add-on.

What to Confirm Before Ordering

  • Which functions are standard vs. optional add-ons for your chosen model
  • Motor brand and endurance cycle rating (10,000 cycles minimum for residential)
  • Wireless protocol (RF, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi) and white-label app availability
  • Electrical configuration for your destination market (plug type, voltage)
  • Whether any features affect MOQ or lead time
