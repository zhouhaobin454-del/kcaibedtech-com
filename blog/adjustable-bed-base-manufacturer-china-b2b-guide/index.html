How to Evaluate an Adjustable Bed Manufacturer from China

By Kang Cai Smart Sleep · Published November 14, 2025

Quick Summary

A practical checklist for B2B buyers evaluating Chinese adjustable bed manufacturers — factory credentials, product range, OEM capability, quality control and communication.

Choosing an adjustable bed manufacturer is a long-term supply chain decision. The right supplier supports product development, quality control, and logistics across multiple order cycles. The wrong one creates costly problems at exactly the wrong time. This checklist helps B2B buyers evaluate Chinese adjustable bed manufacturers before committing to an OEM or ODM program.

1. Factory Credentials and History

Request basic factory credentials before engaging further:

  • Business registration and export license
  • Years of operation (longer = more stable supply chain)
  • Factory size in m² (larger = more production capacity)
  • Number of employees and engineers on staff

Kang Cai was established in 2004, operates from a 25,000 m² facility in Shatian Town, Dongguan, and employs 100 team members with 50+ engineers. This baseline confirms a serious, long-operating manufacturer rather than a trading company or recent entrant.

2. Product Specialization

Confirm that the supplier specializes in adjustable beds rather than offering them as one of many unrelated product categories. Specialized manufacturers have deeper engineering knowledge, better tooling, and stronger component supplier relationships. Ask to see the full product range and confirm how long each product has been in production.

3. OEM and ODM Capability

Distinguish between OEM (your brand on their product) and ODM (your brand on a product designed or co-designed for you):

  • OEM: confirm minimum order quantities, branding options, and lead time
  • ODM: confirm engineering resources, tooling costs, and development timeline
  • Ask for examples of past OEM/ODM programs (with client permission)

4. Quality Control Process

A serious manufacturer has quality control checkpoints throughout production — not just a final inspection. Ask about:

  • Incoming materials inspection
  • In-process QC (weld inspection, motor testing)
  • Function testing before packing
  • Pre-shipment inspection process
  • Endurance testing specifications (10,000 cycles is the standard minimum)

5. Communication and Support

Evaluate responsiveness and clarity before placing any order. A supplier that takes three days to answer a basic product question will take longer to resolve a production issue. Good indicators:

  • English-speaking sales and technical team
  • Response time to initial inquiry (under 24 hours is standard)
  • Willingness to answer technical questions in detail
  • Availability for video calls or factory visits

6. Sample and Trial Order Policy

Any legitimate manufacturer will offer samples before bulk orders. Confirm sample cost, lead time, and whether samples reflect production-grade quality. Be cautious of suppliers who claim samples cannot be provided or who provide samples that differ significantly from production units.
